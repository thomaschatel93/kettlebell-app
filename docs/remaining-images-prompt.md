# Prompts for the remaining exercise images

Thirteen movements still need a still. Eleven are single-position and fit one grid.
Two are two-position and need their own image, because side-by-side panels need
horizontal room that a grid cell cannot give them.

Ask for the largest canvas offered. Landscape, 1536×1024, suits the 4×3 grid.

---

## PROMPT 1 — the grid (copy everything below the line)

Create one image containing a 4-column by 3-row grid of flat vector fitness
illustrations. Twelve cells, read left to right, top to bottom.

STYLE, identical in every cell:
- A single athletic male figure, clean bold black outlines, flat colour fill, no
  gradients, no shading beyond one simple highlight.
- Bright blue short-sleeve t-shirt, black athletic shorts with a single white side
  stripe, red-and-white trainers, short brown hair.
- Matte black cast-iron kettlebell with one soft grey highlight on the bell.
- Pure white background throughout, including the gutters between cells.
- Side or three-quarter view, whichever shows the movement most clearly.
- Every figure drawn at the SAME scale, so no cell looks zoomed in or out relative
  to the others.
- Generous, even white space between cells and even margins at the edges.
- **No text anywhere. No labels, no captions, no numbers, no watermark.**

ANATOMY, in every cell:
- Five fingers per hand.
- The hand grips the kettlebell HANDLE, never the round ball of the bell.
- The wrist stays straight, in line with the forearm, wherever the bell is held.
- A neutral spine unless the movement requires otherwise. No rounded lower back.

THE TWELVE CELLS:

1. Kettlebell Clean, side view. The bell caught in the rack: forearm vertical
   against the ribs, elbow tucked tight to the body, the bell resting on the back
   of the forearm, wrist straight, standing tall with soft knees.

2. Kettlebell Clean, front view. The same rack position seen from the front, so the
   elbow tucked against the ribs and the straight wrist are both clearly visible.

3. Push Press. Mid-drive: a slight knee dip just finishing as the legs extend, the
   bell travelling upward past the head, the arm not yet locked out.

4. High Pull. The bell at chest height, elbow high and drawn back behind the body,
   hips fully extended, the handle horizontal.

5. Racked Carry. Walking forward with one bell in the rack, forearm vertical, elbow
   glued to the ribs, torso upright and square, shoulders level.

6. Goblet Carry. Walking forward holding one bell by the horns at chest height,
   elbows tucked in, the bell against the chest rather than drifting out in front,
   ribs down and shoulders back.

7. Overhead Carry. Walking forward with one bell locked out overhead, the arm
   vertical over the shoulder, elbow locked, upper arm close to the ear, the free
   arm out for balance, eyes forward rather than up at the bell.

8. Bulgarian Split Squat. Rear foot resting on a low bench behind, front knee bent
   to about ninety degrees and tracking directly over the front foot, one bell held
   in the rack position.

9. Dead Row. Hinged back with a flat back until the torso is near parallel with the
   floor, free hand braced on the thigh, one bell pulled up to the ribs with the
   elbow tracking straight back, the other bell space empty on the floor beside the
   foot.

10. Half-Kneeling Press. Kneeling on one knee with the other foot flat in front,
    both knees at about ninety degrees, pressing one bell straight up from the rack,
    finishing with the arm beside the ear, wrist in line with the forearm.

11. Floor Pullover. Lying on the back, knees bent and feet flat, holding one bell by
    the horns with both hands, arms straight with soft elbows, taking the bell back
    over the head while the lower back stays pressed into the floor and the ribs
    stay down.

12. Figure 8. Athletic stance hinged forward at the hips, the bell passing backwards
    between the legs, one hand meeting it behind the knee to receive it.

## PROMPT 2 — the two-position pair (a separate image)

Same style, anatomy and background rules as above. **No text anywhere.**

Create one image containing two wide cells stacked vertically, each showing two
positions of the same figure side by side with a small black arrow between them.

TOP CELL — Clean and Press. Left: the rack position, forearm vertical against the
ribs, elbow tucked, wrist straight. Right: the overhead lockout, arm straight, bicep
beside the ear, ribs down.

BOTTOM CELL — Snatch. Left: the bell mid-flight rising overhead, the hand punching
up through the handle. Right: the locked-out overhead finish, the bell settled on
the back of the forearm, wrist straight.

---

## Before any of these goes in the app

Check the hands, the grip and the spine on every cell. The last batch produced a
clean where the hand gripped the ball instead of the handle and the elbow flared
away from the ribs — the exact mistake the app's own cue text warns about for that
lift. A picture that contradicts its own caption is worse than no picture.
